12. African and Caribbean nations call for formal apology for transatlantic slavery

非洲和加勒比国家呼吁对跨大西洋奴隶制正式道歉

African and Caribbean countries have called for a formal apology and reparations from countries that benefited from the transatlantic slave trade. The demands come at the end of a three day conference in Ghana which looked to advance the push for reparatory justice. It follows a landmark UN resolution earlier in March which recognised transatlantic slavery as the "gravest crime against humanity", urging UN member states to contribute to a reparations fund. Around 12-15 million African men, women and children were captured and trafficked to the Americas to work as slaves from the 15-19th century. A 19-point reparations plan has been endorsed as part of the "Next Steps" conference in the Ghanaian capital of Accra. It calls for comprehensive debt relief, the restitution of looted cultural property, and the establishment of a global reparations fund, though no specific amount was stated. It also addresses the disproportionate impact of slavery on African women and girls.

非洲和加勒比国家呼吁从跨大西洋奴隶贸易中受益的国家正式道歉并做出赔偿。这些要求是在加纳举行的为期三天的会议结束时提出的，该会议旨在推动赔偿性司法的进程。此前，联合国于三月初通过了一项具有里程碑意义的决议，该决议承认跨大西洋奴隶制是“最严重的危害人类罪”，并敦促联合国成员国为赔偿基金捐款。从 15 世纪到 19 世纪，大约有 12-1500 万非洲男人、女人和儿童被俘虏并被贩运到美洲充当奴隶。作为在加纳首都阿克拉举行的“下一步”会议的一部分，一项 19 点赔偿计划已得到批准。它呼吁全面减免债务，归还被掠夺的文化财产，并建立全球赔偿基金，但没有说明具体数额。它还解决了奴隶制对非洲妇女和女童造成的过度影响。
